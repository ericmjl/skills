# Purpose playbooks

One playbook per purpose from SKILL.md Step 2. Read the matching playbook
BEFORE drafting. wpm bands come from the speech-rate research in
`research-digest.md`; word budgets are band math, recompute at the chosen
length and verify in the Step 7 checklist.

| Purpose | Length | wpm band | Primary metric |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Founder / brand story | 2 to 3 min | 150 to 165 | Trust + signup |
| Landing-page conversion | 60 to 90 s | 165 to 180 | Conversion lift |
| YouTube explainer / education | 5 to 10 min | 160 to 175 | Retention curve |
| Short-form (Shorts/Reels/TikTok) | 15 to 60 s | 175 to 195 | Completion |
| Demo / testimonial | 60 to 120 s | 150 to 170 | Doubt killed |

## Founder / brand story

Convert a stranger's mild interest into trust-based action (waitlist,
signup, reply). The metric is the action, not views.

Structure, by share of word count:

1. 0 to 8 percent: the viewer's tension, second person, the named doubt
   from intake ("If AI can do what took you a decade to learn, what are
   you still for?").
2. 8 to 25 percent: compressed origin story, the real moment the founder
   hit that same tension. Personal and sensory over produced; amateur and
   real transports better than polished commercial narrative (van Laer
   meta-analysis).
3. 25 to 35 percent: re-engagement beat, the conviction thesis as its own
   short sentence.
4. 35 to 70 percent: proof, what was built or tried, with one owned
   number. The hook's number test reprises here as a receipt.
5. 70 to 90 percent: the offer. What it is, who it is for, honest
   exclusions ("not for everybody"); perceived manipulative intent or
   heavy brand presence collapses transportation.
6. Final 10 to 20 percent: close the macro loop, one CTA, warm, one
   specific next action (peak-end colors the whole watch).

Voice: the founder's spoken register (pair with a voice skill); table
read required. Production: face-forward thumbnail, click-to-play beside
the signup CTA. Promise only what the offerer controls.

## Landing-page conversion

Kill the ONE named doubt between headline and CTA. Video is
conversion-neutral on average across ~35k landing pages; it pays only as
a doubt-killer. A/B test rather than assume lift.

Structure:

1. 0 to 10 percent: open on the doubt itself, as a question.
2. 10 to 45 percent: mechanism, how it works in one concrete pass.
3. 45 to 80 percent: receipt, a demo fragment or one customer number.
4. 80 to 100 percent: CTA handoff; the last line names the next action
   and the button is visible on screen when the video ends.

Placement mechanics (all measured, see digest): click-to-play over
autoplay (41.2 vs 9.7 percent CVR); lightbox beats inline beats no video
(13.0 / 11.0 / 6.5 percent); captions mandatory (92 percent of mobile
viewers muted; captioned videos 80 percent more likely to complete);
never gate the video behind an email ask; lazy-load via thumbnail
facade; keep the CTA above the fold at 375px; compress 720p H.264/H.265.

Length: engagement peaks near 60 seconds and decays past 120; hold the
60 to 90 second ceiling. One documented cut from 4 minutes to 90 seconds
lifted conversion from 2.1 to 8.3 percent. One macro loop (the doubt
resolved), micro-payoffs every 10 to 15 seconds.

## YouTube explainer / education

Hold a retention CURVE (a smooth 50 percent beats a cliffy 50 percent;
cliffs predict where future viewers leave) while delivering the title's
exact promise.

Structure:

1. Hook: engage the exact title/thumbnail promise inside 15 seconds (87
   percent of hits do), concrete claim plus one number, never a greeting
   or context dump.
2. Macro loop: an explanatory question ("I know what, not why" gaps pull
   2 to 3x longer than surface gaps). Renew or resolve every 3 to 4
   minutes.
3. Sections: under 300 words each; ceiling of 4 to 5 new concepts per
   minute; adversative transitions ("but", "the catch") over additive
   ones.
4. At 25 to 35 percent: deliberate re-engagement beat (stakes
   escalation, specificity injection, new loop, perspective shift).
5. Back half: density is affordable there; viewers past halfway have
   committed (sunk cost).
6. End: best material near the end, macro loop closed in the final 20
   percent, end on one specific next action. Never closings, gratitude,
   or subscribe prompts: those are the most-skipped segments in a
   39,008-video skip analysis.

wpm 160 to 175: faster-speaking instructors engage more (edX data), but
viewers already play content at 180 to 280 effective wpm, so speed up
the CUT, not the speaker. Benchmarks: 50 to 60 percent average retention
is strong at 5 to 10 minutes. Diagnose cliffs in the first 30 seconds
(hook) or at the 25 to 35 percent mark (missing beat); fix that moment,
not the whole script.

## Short-form (Shorts/Reels/TikTok)

Completion and rewatch in 15 to 60 seconds. There is no back half:
front-load everything.

- Hook: frame 1 is visual (the thing itself, mid-action); line 1 is a
  content hook (claim, number, or question). "Hook in 3 seconds" applies
  to the VISUAL hook here.
- Loop: withhold the payoff until the final 20 percent ("wait for it"
  done honestly); the payoff must land inside the video, never a caption
  or part 2.
- Density: zero throat-clearing; every line carries new information.
- Edit rhythm: seamless jump cuts raise liking and fluency; overlapping
  cuts help sustained attention only at low-to-moderate frequency; keep
  scenes at or above 1 second so they can encode.
- Captions and text overlay are mandatory (feeds default to sound-off).
- CTA: exactly one, spoken or overlay, never in a preamble.
- Benchmarks: treat the top of the under-5-minute band (65 to 75 percent
  completion or better) as the target; sudden drop-offs are hook
  failures.
- Honesty: baited hooks cliff and platforms cap accounts that pattern.

## Demo / testimonial

Kill a specific adoption doubt with evidence on camera.

Demo: the task start to finish, with the ONE friction moment (the named
doubt) resolved on camera in real time. Jump-cut the boring middle,
slow down only at the doubt moment. Short voiceover lines; captions.

Testimonial: the customer's own words, a before-to-after arc, one
specific number from their mouth. Never script it: perceived
manipulative intent collapses transportation, and light brand presence
beats heavy. Raw and slightly imperfect wins.

Benchmarks (multi-study synthesis, see digest): testimonial 39 to 61
percent midpoint lift, explainer ~50 percent, demo ~18 percent; ambient
background loops are ~0 or negative on conversion pages (reserve them
for brand pages). Length 60 to 120 seconds; cut demos harder than feels
comfortable.
