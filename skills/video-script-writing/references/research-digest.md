## Hook studies

**PrePublish 349-hook study (2026-07, prepublish.ai/blog/youtube-hook-study).**
177 overperformers vs 172 underperformers across 36 scripted talking-head
channels, 254M combined views; first 45 seconds of captions classified per
video.

- Hits open with a concrete claim 33.3% vs 25.0%; contain a specific number
  63.3% vs 52.3%; engage the title's exact promise 87.0% vs 77.9%.
- Flops: context dumps 26.2% vs 18.6%; greetings 11.6% vs 7.3%; 16.3% of
  flops never give any reason to keep watching (vs 10.2% of hits).
- NULL findings: median wpm 181 vs 180; first concrete reason at word 14 vs
  15. Pace and speed-to-point separate nothing within the normal band.
- Robustness: under per-channel paired tests, three gaps survive (title
  promise p=.013, never-a-reason p=.021, claim-first p=.024); the number and
  greeting gaps weaken to noise. Treat numbers as suggestive, not law.
- Scope: scripted talking-head niches only; first 45s is one window.

**Practical 15-second deadline (PrePublish script checklist, 2026-03).**
Not a measured threshold, a drafting rule: about as long as a viewer extends
credit to a stranger's video. "Hook in 3 seconds" is right for a VISUAL hook
only; a content hook (claim, result, question, stake) needs more room. A
promise of value does not count; it asks for credit instead of giving a
reason.

## Retention benchmarks and curve shape

- Strong average retention by length (2026 benchmark synthesis, citing
  Backlinko's 1.3M-video analysis among others): under 5 min 65 to 75%;
  5 to 10 min 50 to 60%; 10 to 15 min 40 to 50%; 15 to 30 min 35 to 45%.
  Education and tech niches sit high in bracket; gaming/reaction low.
- Retention Rabbit (May 2025, 150M+ minutes): platform-wide average 23.7%;
  only 16.8% of videos exceed 50%; 55%+ of total drop-off happens in the
  first 60 seconds; intros holding 65%+ past the first minute correlate with
  58% higher average view duration afterward.
- Curve SHAPE matters as much as the average: a smooth curve at 50% beats a
  cliff-then-flat curve at 50%, because cliffs predict where future viewers
  leave. Diagnose cliffs, not averages.
- Most retention problems are diagnosable in the first 30 seconds (hook) or
  at the 25 to 35 percent mark (missing re-engagement beat).
- vRetention (WWW 2024; 229,178 retention curves from YouTube/Bilibili).
  Viewers commonly seek, skip, and replay rather than watch linearly.
  5-gram analysis of 39,008 YouTube videos: the most-skipped segments are
  closings ("see you in the next"), gratitude ("thank you so much for"),
  and subscribe prompts. Never spend the ending on thanks or subs.

## Curiosity and open loops

- Loewenstein (1994), information-gap theory: curiosity is an aversive
  drive state born of a specific gap between what you know and want to
  know. Inverted-U versus prior knowledge (zero context = no gap; near-full
  knowledge = gap too small). You must SUPPLY enough context to make the
  gap visible before exploiting it. Situational inducers: a posed question;
  a sequence with anticipated but unknown resolution; an expectation
  violation; information others possess; challenged prior beliefs.
- Zeigarnik (1927): interrupted tasks are recalled far better than
  completed ones (~90% in the original line of experiments); unresolved
  questions occupy working memory and create tension until resolved.
  Translation: open loops sustain attention; closed loops release it.
- Loop craft (synthesis across PrePublish watch-time psychology, the Loop
  Ladder, and the curiosity-gap literature):
  - Explanatory gaps ("I know what, not why") pull 2 to 3x longer than
    surface gaps ("what happens next"). Predictive gaps sit between.
  - A single gap holds pull roughly 3 to 4 minutes; past 5 minutes without
    partial resolution, curiosity curdles into frustration.
  - Max 2 concurrent open loops; more overloads working memory.
  - Close a micro-loop every 10 to 15 seconds (a number, a reveal, a
    partial answer) while opening the next; a single front-loaded loop
    leaves a dead middle.
  - Close the main loop in the final 20 percent; closing it early collapses
    the motivation to continue.
  - Every loop must close inside the video. An unpaid loop reads as
    betrayal, and the memory of betrayal carries to the NEXT video.
  - Clickbait vs honest loop: mechanically identical at second one,
    opposite at second five. The retention cliff after a baited hook is the
    tell, and platforms cap accounts that pattern.

## Cognitive load and script pacing

- Cognitive load above 4 to 5 new concepts per minute multiplies exit
  probability (~3.2x in PrePublish's synthesis).
- Break any section running past ~300 words; vary sentence lengths;
  adversative transitions ("but", "the catch is") hold attention better
  than additive ones ("also", "next").
- The 25 to 35 percent stretch is the known flat zone: opening momentum has
  faded, commitment has not yet set in. Place a deliberate re-engagement
  beat there (stakes escalation, specificity injection, open loop,
  perspective shift, social proof). One sentence is usually enough.
- Peak-end rule: the ending colors memory of the whole watch. Best material
  near the end; end warm, on one specific next action.
- Sunk cost protects the back half: viewers past the halfway mark have
  decided the video was worth it; you can afford density there that you
  cannot at minute one.

## Speech rate

- Rodero (2019, Journal of Advertising Research; 160/180/200 wpm audio
  ads): 180 wpm won on recall, recognition, arousal, and valence; 160 was
  WORST. Curvilinear, not "slower is clearer."
- Kim, Allen & Preiss (2019, meta-analysis of 44 comparisons): persuasion
  is maximal at moderate speech rates; moderate-fast reads as credible,
  knowledgeable, charismatic (Berger et al. 2017); slow reads as lower
  competence (Smith et al. 1975).
- Educational-video analytics (AAEE 2025, 250+ videos): retention rises
  with speaking rate up to an optimum then declines; viewers commonly
  play content at 180 to 280 effective wpm regardless, so editors should
  speed up the CUT, not the speaker. Guo et al. (2014, edX): students
  engaged MORE with faster-speaking instructors.
- Working band for scripted talking-head video: roughly 150 to 180 wpm
  depending on purpose (see playbooks). Overload thresholds in compressed
  speech research sit near 250 to 300 wpm; do not chase them.

## Editing, cuts, and visual attention

- Scene cuts and complexity (Journal of the Academy of Marketing Science,
  2026; eye-tracking, 2,520 viewings of 42 PSAs): every extra second of
  scene duration drops attentional synchrony ~5%; a one-SD jump in visual
  complexity drops it ~12 to 26% long-run; a cut RAISES it ~40% long-run,
  peaking about 0.66 s AFTER the cut. Implications: put key content at the
  START of scenes, not the end; hold important centered elements at least
  1 second; keep frames simple; cuts have diminishing returns and scenes
  under ~1 second cannot encode.
- Editing density and time perception (Cognition/Santero line): fast-paced
  editing makes time subjectively fly (good for watch-through) while
  durations feel fuller; both edits of perception raise engagement markers.
- Cuts and blink-suppression (Andreu-Sánchez et al., EEG): blink rate (an
  attention marker) drops significantly in the second after a cut; chaotic
  editing styles increase visual-cortex engagement. Attention follows
  change.
- Jump-cut styles (Marketing Trends Congress 2026, N=242, 20s talking-head
  clips benchmarked against 50 best-practice TikToks): SEAMLESS cuts
  (dead-air removal, continuity preserved) raise liking/fluency; OVERLAPPING
  cuts (slight audio overlaps) raise sustained attention but ONLY at low-to
  -moderate frequency; higher pacing reduced sustained engagement overall.
  Do not chase cut-speed for its own sake.
- Practical edit rhythm: a visual change every 4 to 7 seconds for
  talking-head content; micro-zooms and overlays count; no static 5-second
  windows; pair cuts with the narration beat they emphasize.

## Narrative transportation

- Green & Brock (2000, JPSP, 4 experiments): transportation (absorption
  into a story: imagery + affect + attentional focus) increases
  story-consistent beliefs and protagonist liking; transportation reduces
  counterarguing (highly transported readers find fewer "false notes");
  fact-vs-fiction labeling barely matters. Being absorbed, not being
  lectured, is what moves beliefs.
- van Laer et al. (2019, Journal of Business Research; meta-analysis, 64
  articles, 138 effect sizes): in digital settings, USER-generated
  (amateur, personal) stories transport better than professional ones;
  transportation effects persist and can increase over time (Appel &
  Richter 2007). A real person on camera beats produced polish.
- Thomas & Grigsby (systematic review, 95 articles): story format vs
  expository/list format reliably increases transportation; perceived
  manipulative intent or heavy brand presence REDUCES it. High-involvement
  decisions (retreats, courses, B2B) need imagined experience, which
  feature lists cannot provide.
- Escalas & Stern / sensory-language line: concrete, sensory language
  drives the imagery that drives transportation; abstract claims keep the
  viewer at a distance.

## Landing-page video and conversion

- Placement (Unbounce/Vidyard case): lightbox click-to-play 13.0% CVR vs
  inline embed 11.0% vs no-video 6.5% (100% vs 69% lift). Lightbox wins by
  focusing attention and keeping the CTA visible when the video ends.
- The honest baseline (Unbounce analysis of ~35k landing pages): pages
  WITH video converted the same or slightly WORSE overall, across
  industries, channels, and devices. The famous "86% lift" traces to an
  old unsourced vendor stat. Video pays only when it kills a specific
  doubt; otherwise it is page weight. A/B test, never assume.
- Sound and captions (Verizon Media/Publicis): 92% of mobile viewers and
  83% overall watch with sound off; 69% mute in public; captioned videos
  are 80% more likely to be watched to completion. Burn in captions.
- Click-to-play vs autoplay (Vmaker A/B): 41.2% vs 9.7% conversion.
  Sound-on autoplay is blocked by browsers and hated when it works. Custom
  thumbnail with a human face out-plays logo cards.
- Length (Wistia engagement curve across 13M videos): engagement peaks
  near 60 seconds and decays past 120. One documented test: cutting a
  demo from 4 minutes to 90 seconds lifted conversion 2.1% to 8.3%.
- Mechanical rules: never let the video push the CTA below the fold on a
  375px viewport; lazy-load via thumbnail facade (raw iframe embeds can
  drag 800KB+ of scripts and wreck LCP, worst on mobile); do not gate the
  video behind an email ask; one CTA per page; compress aggressively
  (H.264/H.265, 720p for mobile).
- By type (multi-study synthesis): explainer ~50% midpoint lift, testimonial
  39 to 61%, demo ~18%, background ambient ~0 or negative on conversion
  pages (reserve ambient loops for brand pages).
